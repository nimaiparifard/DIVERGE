GNN-Only Graph Structure Enhancement (No LLM)
==============================================
For Related Work in template.tex — papers that refine/rewire/denoise graph topology
using GNNs or classical node features, WITHOUT LLMs as enhancers.

Your DIVERGE extensions (Methods II & III) sit at this intersection:
  • Method II: remove edges with low cosine similarity between LLM node embeddings
    → post-hoc, feature-similarity pruning to increase homophily
  • Method III: GNN edge predictor with uncertainty to identify noisy edges
    → learned, task-driven edge removal

Use these papers to position DIVERGE as building on GSL tradition but using
rich LLM semantics (not raw BoW/PLM features) for structure refinement.

── TIER 1: Foundational & closest to your cosine-similarity pruning ──

1. IDGL — Iterative Deep Graph Learning for Graph Neural Networks
   Full title: Iterative Deep Graph Learning for Graph Neural Networks: Better and Robust Node Embeddings
   Venue: NeurIPS 2020 (published)
   Authors: Chen et al.
   What it does: Iteratively learns graph structure as weighted COSINE SIMILARITY of
     node embeddings and refines embeddings on the updated graph until convergence.
   Why relevant: Closest classical analogue to your Method II — both use feature
     similarity to revise edges, but IDGL is end-to-end iterative GSL on raw features,
     while DIVERGE applies a thresholded pruning step on LLM embeddings post-hoc.
   Enhancement type: Joint iterative structure + representation learning

2. ProGNN — Graph Structure Learning for Robust Graph Neural Networks
   Venue: KDD 2020 (published)
   Authors: Jin et al.
   What it does: Jointly learns a clean adjacency matrix and GNN weights using
     sparsity, low-rank, and feature-smoothness regularizers to defend noisy/adversarial edges.
   Why relevant: Canonical GSL denoising paper; motivates that real graphs violate
     homophily/smoothness and benefit from structure correction before message passing.
   Enhancement type: Learned adjacency denoising (regularization-based)

3. GPS — Towards Understanding and Reducing Graph Structural Noise for GNNs
   Venue: ICML 2023 (published)
   Authors: Dong & Kluger
   What it does: Proposes ESNR metric for measuring structural noise; rewires graphs
     via Graph Propensity Score (GPS) from self-supervised link prediction using node features.
   Why relevant: Explicitly frames feature-aware edge removal; supports your claim that
     noisy edges hurt GNNs and that feature similarity/propensity can guide rewiring.
   Enhancement type: Feature-aware graph rewiring (pre-processing)

4. PTDNet — Learning to Drop: Robust Graph Neural Network via Topological Denoising
   Venue: WSDM 2021 (published)
   Authors: Luo et al.
   What it does: Learns a parameterized network to DROP task-irrelevant edges end-to-end
     with the downstream GNN; uses nuclear-norm sparsity on the pruned graph.
   Why relevant: Direct "learning to remove edges" counterpart to your threshold-based
     pruning; task-driven rather than fixed cosine threshold.
   Enhancement type: Learned edge dropping (end-to-end denoising)

── TIER 2: Joint structure learning & edge prediction ──

5. LDS — Learning Discrete Structures for Graph Neural Networks
   Venue: ICML 2019 (published)
   Authors: Franceschi et al.
   What it does: Bilevel optimization that learns a discrete edge distribution jointly
     with GCN parameters; handles missing, incomplete, or noisy graphs.
   Why relevant: Foundational GSL work cited by nearly all follow-ups; good one-line
     anchor for the "jointly learn structure + GNN" paradigm.
   Enhancement type: Discrete structure learning

6. GRCN — Graph-Revised Convolutional Network
   Venue: ECML-PKDD 2020 (published)
   Authors: Yu et al.
   What it does: Two coupled GNN modules — one revises edges (add/reweight) from node
     embeddings, one classifies on the revised graph — optimized jointly.
   Why relevant: Parallel to your Method III (encoder–decoder edge predictor + GNN);
     GRCN revises structure inside training, DIVERGE uses uncertainty as a separate stage.
   Enhancement type: Graph revision module + classification module

7. GAug — Data Augmentation for Graph Neural Networks
   Venue: AAAI 2021 (published)
   Authors: Zhao et al.
   What it does: Graph autoencoder predicts edge existence; adds likely edges and drops
     unlikely ones to augment structure for downstream GNN training.
   Why relevant: Edge-prediction-based structure modification; complements your
     uncertainty-based edge existence scoring in Method III.
   Enhancement type: Edge prediction for augmentation/pruning

── TIER 3: Self-supervised / robust structure refinement ──

8. STABLE — Reliable Representations Make A Stronger Defender: Unsupervised Structure Refinement for Robust GNN
   Venue: KDD 2022 (published)
   Authors: Li et al.
   What it does: Self-supervised contrastive learning to obtain reliable node embeddings,
     then modifies graph structure by embedding similarity before downstream GNN.
   Why relevant: Unsupervised structure refinement pipeline; similarity-based rewiring
     without labels — analogous spirit to cosine pruning on unlabeled edges.
   Enhancement type: Self-supervised embedding → similarity rewiring

9. CoGSL — Compact Graph Structure Learning via Mutual Information Compression
   Venue: WWW 2022 (published)
   Authors: Fatemi et al.
   What it does: Learns a minimal sufficient graph structure by compressing mutual
     information between multiple graph views while preserving task performance.
   Why relevant: Theoretical framing for "what is a good refined graph" — task-relevant
     edges only, noise compressed away.
   Enhancement type: Multi-view MI compression

10. SUBLIME — Towards Unsupervised Deep Graph Structure Learning
    Venue: WWW 2022 (published)
    Authors: Liu et al.
    What it does: Unsupervised GSL via structure bootstrapping contrastive learning
      between a learned graph and an anchor graph.
    Why relevant: Shows structure can be improved without label supervision on edges.
    Enhancement type: Unsupervised contrastive GSL

── TIER 4: Homophily / heterophily rewiring (supporting motivation) ──

11. ES-GNN — Generalizing Graph Neural Networks Beyond Homophily With Edge Splitting
    Venue: IEEE TPAMI 2024 (published)
    Authors: Li et al.
    What it does: Splits edges into homophilic vs. heterophilic subgraphs and propagates
      separately to disentangle task-relevant from harmful connections.
    Why relevant: Formalizes that not all edges help GNNs; some should be treated as noise
      — supports your noisy-edge removal motivation.
    Enhancement type: Edge splitting (not pure deletion)

12. DIGL — Predict then Propagate: Graph Neural Networks meet Personalized PageRank
    Venue: ICLR 2019 (published)
    Authors: Klicpera et al.
    What it does: Diffusion-based graph denoising (personalized PageRank) before GNN;
      smooths/sparsifies adjacency to reduce noise propagation.
    Why relevant: Classic pre-GSL denoising baseline; often compared against in GSL papers.
    Enhancement type: Diffusion-based structure smoothing

    Venue: WWW 2021 (published)
    Authors: Wang et al.
    What it does: Estimates graph structure from node features via metric learning,

    Why relevant: Empirical evidence that citation graphs contain many dispensable edges —

    Enhancement type: Learned edge mask sparsification

HOW TO CONTRAST WITH YOUR DIVERGE STRUCTURAL ENHANCER (for Research Gap)
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  GNN-only GSL (ProGNN, IDGL, PTDNet, …):
    • Revise structure from raw/shallow node features (BoW, bag-of-words, PLM frozen embeddings)
    • Often joint end-to-end optimization — entangles structure learning with a single GNN
    • No access to diverse semantic views from multiple fine-tuned LLMs

  LLM enhancers (TAPE, RoSE, GraphEdit, …):
    • Use LLMs to rewrite text OR structure before GNN training
    • API cost, hallucination risk, multi-stage pipelines

  DIVERGE + Semantic Structure Enhancer (your contribution):
    • Native text only (no LLM rewriting); diverse LLM encodings provide multiple semantic views
    • Post-hoc structure refinement: cosine similarity pruning (Method II) or GNN uncertainty (Method III)
    • Combines multi-view LLM semantics with GSL-inspired homophily enhancement — a middle path

\subsection{Edge Rewiring or Denoising in Graph Learning}
Graph Structure Learning (GSL) aims to recover a task-favorable topology when the
observed graph is noisy, incomplete, or misaligned with node features
\citep{zhu2021survey}. Representative methods jointly optimize the adjacency and GNN
parameters: \textbf{LDS} \citep{franceschi2019lds} learns a discrete edge distribution
via bilevel optimization; \textbf{ProGNN} \citep{jin2020prognn} regularizes a learned
graph toward sparsity, low rank, and feature smoothness; and \textbf{IDGL}
\citep{chen2020idgl} iteratively refines structure through cosine similarity of node
embeddings. Closer to explicit denoising, \textbf{PTDNet} \citep{luo2021ptdnet} learns
to drop task-irrelevant edges, \textbf{GPS} \citep{dong2023gps} rewires graphs using
feature-based link propensity scores, and \textbf{GRCN}/\textbf{GAug}
\citep{yu2020grcn,zhao2021gaug} revise or augment edges via learned edge predictors.
Self-supervised refiners such as \textbf{STABLE} \citep{li2022stable},
\textbf{CoGSL} \citep{fatemi2022cogsl}, and \textbf{SUBLIME} \citep{liu2022sublime}
further improve robustness without relying on LLMs. These methods establish that
pruning or rewiring noisy edges can substantially improve message passing, but they
typically operate on shallow node features rather than semantically rich LLM embeddings.

\subsection{Edge Denoising for Text-Attributed Graphs}
On text-attributed graphs, early work such as \textbf{VHE} \citep{yang2019vhe} modeled
homophily between linked documents in a generative framework, while standard GSL methods
(\textbf{GEN}, \textbf{GLCN}) have been evaluated on citation networks with bag-of-words
features \citep{wang2021gen,jiang2019glcn}. More recently, LLM-based enhancers modify
node text or topology before GNN training (Section~\ref{sec:llm-enhancers}). In contrast,
our structural enhancer keeps native text unchanged and uses diverse LLM \emph{encodings}
only to score edge reliability—combining GSL-style homophily enhancement with the
multi-view semantic diversity of DIVERGE.
